### About me

I am an avid conservation geneticist that utilizes multiple genomic techniques to 
understand marine populations, such as oysters in my graduate students and tuna in my postdoctoral studies.
My research is at the intersection of population and conservation genomics under environmental change 
and quantitative and theoretical marine ecology. I am passionate about contributing to and utilizing
open, computational approaches to genomic discovery.

I am currently a postdoc in the Mamoozadeh lab (Dr. Nadya Mamoozadeh) at North Carolina State University, where I utilize 
genomic tools for rapid identification of tuna species that are of high economic importance. Additionally, 
I use low-coverage whole genome sequencing as a tool for population assessment of high-value tuna species.
This work is conducted jointly with Dr. Mariah Meek.

### Education

I completed my PhD in 2025 at the University of New Hampshire under the supervision of Dr. Bonnie Brown in the department of biology. 
Broadly, I studied eastern oyster population genomics and their responses to stressors, such as disease and 
temporal conditions, using the Oxford Nanopore platform. I earned my MSc in 2021 in integrative and organismal biology at the 
University of New Hampshire studying temporal dynamics of eastern oyster larval abundances in Great Bay Estuary as well 
as infection etiology of MSX and Dermo on eastern oysters. I earned my BS in genetics in 2019 at Clemson University, with minors 
in biochemistry and Spanish.
